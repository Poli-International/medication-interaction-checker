## What Is the Medication & Body Art Interaction Checker?

The Medication & Body Art Interaction Checker is a free web tool that helps tattoo artists, piercers, and clients understand how common medications affect body art procedures. The tool presents 7 medication categories containing over 20 specific medications, each with detailed guidance on tattoo healing, piercing healing, bleeding risk, infection susceptibility, and recommended wait times.

When a user selects one or more medications from the checkbox lists, the tool generates individual interaction cards sorted by severity level (high, moderate, low). Each card displays the medication name with its common brand names, a severity badge, separate sections for tattoo and piercing effects, and a wait-time recommendation where applicable. The tool also shows a summary count of selected medications and highlights the number of high-concern items.

The tool covers blood thinners (warfarin, apixaban, aspirin, clopidogrel), retinoids (isotretinoin, tretinoin), corticosteroids (oral, inhaled/topical), immunosuppressants (methotrexate, biologics, ciclosporin), antibiotics (tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones), SSRIs/SNRIs, and other medications (lithium, cancer immunotherapy, diabetes medications).

## Who Should Use This Tool

**Tattoo Artists**, Use during client consultations to identify potential complications before starting a procedure. The tool helps artists understand specific risks like bleeding, poor ink retention, and impaired healing associated with various medications.

**Piercers**, Screen clients for medications that increase bleeding risk or delay fistula formation. The tool provides piercing-specific guidance for each medication.

**Clients**, Check your own medication list before booking an appointment. Understand what information to share with your artist and what questions to ask your doctor.

**Studio Managers**, Incorporate the tool into intake procedures and staff training. Use it to develop consistent policies around medication disclosure and medical clearance requirements.

## How to Use

**Step 1: Locate the medication list.** The tool displays 7 expandable categories on the page: Blood thinners/Anticoagulants, Retinoids, Corticosteroids, Immunosuppressants, Antibiotics, SSRIs/SNRIs, and Other medications.

**Step 2: Select your medications.** Click the checkbox next to each medication you currently take. Each entry shows the generic name and common brand names (e.g., "Warfarin, Coumadin").

**Step 3: Review the results.** After selecting at least one medication, the tool displays a results section showing:
- A summary count of selected medications
- A severity indicator (high, moderate, or low concern)
- Individual interaction cards for each medication

**Step 4: Read each interaction card.** Each card contains:
- Medication name and brand names
- Severity badge (color-coded)
- Tattoo-specific effects
- Piercing-specific effects
- Wait-time guidance (where applicable)

**Step 5: Clear and restart.** Click the "Clear all" button to deselect all medications and start a new check.

## Real Use Cases

### Use Case 1: Client on Blood Thinners

A client selects "Warfarin" from the Blood thinners/Anticoagulants category. The tool displays a high-concern severity badge and explains that significant bleeding during tattooing impairs ink setting, colour saturation, and linework clarity. For piercing, the tool warns of excessive bleeding and prolonged healing. The wait-time guidance advises discussing a supervised pause with the prescribing physician and explicitly states not to discontinue without medical guidance.

### Use Case 2: Client on Isotretinoin

A client selects "Isotretinoin (Accutane/Roaccutane)" from the Retinoids category. The tool shows a high-concern badge and explains that isotretinoin alters skin cell turnover and sebum production, causing unpredictable ink distribution and poor healing. The piercing guidance states that fistula formation is severely impaired. The wait-time recommendation is 6–12 months after completing treatment, with a note to discuss the minimum safe interval with a dermatologist.

### Use Case 3: Artist Screening Multiple Medications

An artist screens a client who selects three medications: "Aspirin (therapeutic dose)" (moderate concern), "SSRIs/SNRIs" (low concern), and "Insulin/Diabetes medications" (moderate concern). The tool shows 3 medications selected with 2 moderate-concern items highlighted. The artist reads that aspirin increases procedural bleeding, SSRIs may cause minor bruising, and diabetes requires stable blood glucose on the day of tattooing. The artist proceeds with the appointment after confirming the client's blood glucose is well-controlled.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1: Is this tool a substitute for medical advice?**
No. This tool is an educational reference only and does not constitute medical advice. Never stop or adjust prescribed medication without consulting your prescribing physician.

**Q2: What if my medication isn't listed?**
The tool covers the most common medication interactions with body art procedures. If your medication isn't listed, inform your artist or piercer and consult your prescribing physician about any potential risks.

**Q3: How accurate are the wait times?**
Wait times are based on standard clinical guidance and professional body art industry standards. Individual factors like dosage, duration of therapy, and personal health status may affect recommendations. Always confirm with your prescribing physician.

**Q4: Can I stop my medication before a procedure?**
Never stop or adjust prescribed medication without consulting your prescribing physician. Some medications (like clopidogrel or biologics) are prescribed for critical health conditions and should not be interrupted for elective procedures.

**Q5: Why do blood thinners affect tattooing?**
Blood thinners increase bleeding during tattooing, which can cause ink to ooze out of the skin, impair colour saturation, and affect linework clarity. Excessive bleeding also creates a poor working surface for the artist.

**Q6: How long after Accutane can I get tattooed?**
Standard guidance recommends waiting 6–12 months after completing isotretinoin before getting tattooed or pierced. Discuss the minimum safe interval with your dermatologist, as individual factors may affect healing.

**Q7: Do topical medications affect piercings?**
Topical medications like tretinoin or topical steroids can affect healing if applied directly to or near a piercing site. The tool recommends discontinuing topical products at the specific site 2–4 weeks before a procedure.

**Q8: What should I tell my artist about my medications?**
Always disclose your full medication list to your artist or piercer, including prescription medications, over-the-counter drugs, and supplements. This information helps them assess risks and plan the procedure safely.

**Q9: Does this tool cover all possible interactions?**
No. The tool covers the most common medication interactions but cannot account for every possible combination or individual health factor. Always consult healthcare professionals for personalized advice.

**Q10: Can I save or print the results?**
The tool does not include a save or print function. You can use your browser's print feature to capture the results, or take notes on the relevant interaction cards.
